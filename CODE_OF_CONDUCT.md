# Code of Conduct

- 中文版：[CODE_OF_CONDUCT.zh-CN.md](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.zh-CN.md)

RouteServer is a technical project with contributors from different companies, countries and levels of
experience. This document describes the behaviour we expect in every project space: issues, pull
requests, review comments, commit messages, discussions, chat and any event organised under the project
name.

It is adapted from the [Contributor Covenant v2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/),
shortened to what we can actually enforce.

## Expected behaviour

- **Discuss the code, not the person.** "This retry loop can spin forever when etcd returns
  `ErrCompacted`" is a review comment. "You clearly don't understand etcd" is not.
- **Assume competence and good faith.** Ask what a change is trying to achieve before concluding it is
  wrong. Most surprising patches have a reason that was never written down.
- **Be precise about disagreement.** State the trade-off, and say what evidence would change your mind.
  A maintainer's decision is a decision, not a verdict on your abilities.
- **Accept that a patch can be declined.** Scope, timing and long-term maintenance cost are legitimate
  reasons to say no, independent of the patch's quality.
- **Respect language and experience differences.** Non-native English and first-time contributors get
  patience, not condescension. Never mock someone's grammar or tooling choices.
- **Respect privacy and confidentiality.** Do not publish other people's private information, and do not
  post material that is not yours to publish — including code from an employer's internal repository.
- **Keep the project neutral.** This is not a venue for political, national or corporate advocacy.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption of discussion.
- Insults, personal or professional attacks, and demeaning comments about someone's competence.
- Discriminatory comments or imagery related to gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability,
  physical appearance, race, ethnicity, religion, nationality, age or level of experience.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing private correspondence or personal data without consent.
- Retaliation against anyone who reports a problem in good faith.
- Deliberately wasting reviewers' time: reopening rejected changes unchanged, editing a pull request to
  remove review context, or submitting generated bulk changes without review capacity.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces and when representing the project publicly — for example in a talk,
a blog post or a comment thread on a third-party site that names RouteServer.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to any maintainer listed in [MAINTAINERS.md](MAINTAINERS.md), by direct
message rather than in the public thread. If the report concerns a maintainer, report it to a different
maintainer; the report is handled without that person in the conversation.

Include what happened, where, when, and who was involved. Links and screenshots help.

Maintainers keep reports confidential and do not share the reporter's identity without permission.

## Enforcement

Maintainers investigate and decide. Responses escalate with severity and repetition:

1. **Private clarification** — a word about what was inappropriate, with no further action.
2. **Public correction** — a maintainer asks for a change of tone in the thread.
3. **Warning** — a written warning recording the behaviour and the expected change.
4. **Temporary suspension** — loss of review, merge or discussion privileges for a stated period.
5. **Permanent ban** — for severe or repeated violations.

Immediate removal of a comment or a pull request may happen in any case, with an explanation. Decisions
are made by the maintainers, by simple majority; the person involved does not participate. The reporter
is informed of the outcome at the level of detail that does not compromise others' privacy. Appeals go
to the maintainers as a group, once.
